Blind Memos02

Sun Tzu

Terrain · Timing · Indirect strategy
Diagnosis
A buyer in motion is a buyer paying premium. 45 days is your window of leverage, not theirs — once they know you have an alternative, the question shifts from whether they pay more to how much.
Recommended move
Do not accept Acquirer A’s first number. Reveal Acquirer B exists to A privately and let them compete on terms, not just price.
Failure mode
You announce competition publicly. Both buyers walk to preserve their own reputational position. You burn 30 of your 45 days with no alternative.
Confidence · High

Machiavelli

Power · Appearances · Incentives
Diagnosis
Your cofounder doesn’t want to wait because the offer is wrong. Your cofounder wants to wait because her payoff vests differently than yours. Discover the misalignment before you negotiate with anyone external.
Recommended move
A private hour with the cap table. Then a private hour with the cofounder. Then the buyers. Internal alignment is a precondition for external leverage — reversed order, both buyers smell uncertainty in seven days.
Failure mode
You assume aligned incentives. You go to A with a counter. Cofounder learns from a board observer and surfaces objection in front of the buyer. Deal collapses on appearances, not on price.
Confidence · High

Maimonides

Systems · Ethics · The golden mean
Diagnosis
The team you built is the asset both acquirers are actually buying. Price-optimizing erases the team in 18 months; term-optimizing keeps the mission alive but at a number that looks worse on paper.
Recommended move
Weight non-price terms above pure euros: retention guarantees, founder roles for 24 months, mission continuity clauses. Counter A on terms; the price moves second.
Failure mode
You take the higher number, blow up the team six months in, and watch the integration unwind. The acquirer writes off the deal. Your reputation in the next venture is the one that pays the bill.
Confidence · High directional · Medium tactical

Schelling

Bargaining · Commitment · BATNA
Diagnosis
Your real BATNA is “can you survive another fundraise,” not “shut down.” If a bridge round is available at any terms, you have walk-away power and the buyers know it; if not, your deadline is a wishbone, not a threat.
Recommended move
Commit publicly to a 14-day decision window only if you have a credible bridge option you can name to yourself. The commitment device only works when the threat is credible to both sides.
Failure mode
You set a 14-day deadline. Acquirer A calls the bluff. You blink on day 14. Your next negotiation in life is conducted from a position both sides remember.
Confidence · High

Kahneman

Bias correction · Base rates
Outlier
Diagnosis
The first offer is anchoring everyone. Loss aversion is making “shut down” feel like a 10x worse outcome than it actually is — founder reputations survive shutdowns regularly, especially when proximate cause is “disciplined walk from bad terms.”
Recommended move
Before any external move, write the post-mortem of each acceptance scenario, dated 18 months out. Most likely failure of accepting A: integration friction, team attrition by month 9. Most likely failure of accepting B: earnout never pays, founder role evaporates by month 12. Then choose against the less-tolerable failure.
Failure mode
Post-hoc rationalization. You write the post-mortem after you’ve already made the choice and it tells you what you wanted to hear.
Confidence · High
